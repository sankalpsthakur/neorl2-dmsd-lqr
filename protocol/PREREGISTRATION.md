## 1. Target
Normalised score on NeoRL-2 DMSD (BARS.md section 2): training data = the official train file (100,000 transitions) and the official validation file (20,000 transitions; used only for model-rollout selection) [wording per Amendment 1]; evaluation = the official simulator `gym.make("DMSD")` (mode `train`) from the pinned repository commit; 1000 evaluation episodes for each of the seeds 7, 42, 210. **Bar: 78.65 (EDAC, Table 2).** The model is a classical controller identified from the data, not an offline-RL algorithm, and says so everywhere.

The model: least-squares linear system identification, then a discrete LQR with target feed-forward and a saturation at the action bounds. 24 identified numbers (A 4x4, B 4x2) and a 12-number controller (K 2x4, F 2x2). The numbers are in normalised action units (force = 30 x action).

## 2. Claim rule
Primary statistic: for each seed s in {7, 42, 210}, the mean normalised score of the frozen controller over its 1000 official-simulator episodes (episode seeds: `ep_seed(s, i) = 100,000,000 + 100,000 s + i`, i = 0..999, passed to `env.reset(seed=...)`). The controller is deterministic, so **the seeds only index three independent sets of evaluation episodes**; there is no training randomness, and the mean +- standard deviation over the three seed means (the statistic of the published table) is reported but is not a test.

The words in item 3 (as replaced by Amendment 1, A1.1) may be used only if all of these hold (n = 1000, the seed set and the draw count are enforced in code, Amendment 1 A1.3):
1. **G0 passed** (section 5, all three parts).
2. The frozen configuration is the one committed before the final kernel was pushed (sha256 check inside the kernel), and all 3 x 1000 episodes finished (no episode dropped, no episode re-run).
3. For each of the three seeds, the lower end of the 95 % percentile-bootstrap interval of its 1000 episode scores (10,000 draws, resampling episodes) is **above 80.95 = 78.65 + 2.30**, the bar plus the bar's own reported spread (BARS.md section 1: the +- is a three-seed sample sd of 2.30).
4. The numpy replica and the official simulator agreed on every episode of the final run (per-episode return difference at most 0.001; the final kernel scores every episode with both).

The published number has no per-episode data, so this is a one-sample comparison against a scalar; no paired test against EDAC exists. [The original sentence here claiming that the rule "cannot pass by chance", with a probability, is WITHDRAWN by Amendment 1, A1.4: it ignored that the bar is itself a best-of-grid number chosen on the evaluation simulator.] A mean above 78.65 whose interval does not clear 80.95 is reported as "above the bar, not by the pre-registered margin".

## 4. Protocol P-DMSD-1 (the corrected protocol; the defect it removes)
The published protocol chooses each baseline's hyper-parameters by the online return on the evaluation simulator (BARS.md section 1), uses the same dynamics for training and testing (mass 20; the mass-30 `test` mode is unused), evaluates unseeded episodes, and labels a sample sd as a standard error. P-DMSD-1, which our rows follow and which others can adopt, is:
1. **Data.** The official Hub parquet files at revision d528956f2d4c3aaefe7314f5615307214841bbcc (sha256 in `protocol/data_manifest.json`): train 100,000 rows to fit, val 20,000 rows to select. Nothing else from the benchmark is read.
2. **Identification** on train only: unstructured least squares of `next_obs[:4]` on `[obs[:4], action]` (no intercept, no known physics, no structure). Audit also fits an intercept and a structured variant; they are reported, not used.
3. **Design:** discrete LQR on the identified (A, B), Q = diag(qp, qp, qv, qv), R = I (action units), gain by iterating the Riccati recursion to convergence; target feed-forward `a_ff = F tgt` with F = pinv(B) (I - A)[:, :2] from the identified model; control `a = clip(F tgt - K (x - [tgt, 0]), -1, 1)`.
4. **Selection without the simulator:** a fixed grid qp in {0.1, 1, 10, 100, 1e3, 1e4, 1e5} x qv in {0.001, 0.01, 0.1, 1, 10} (35 candidates, R = I; larger R is redundant by scaling). Each candidate is scored by the mean model-rollout return over the **200 validation episodes** (start state and target sequence taken from the val parquet, dynamics = the identified model, saturation applied, reward = the DMSD reward in float64). The best mean wins; ties within 1e-9 go to the smaller qp, then the smaller qv. Candidates whose closed loop is not stable (spectral radius of A - BK at least 1) are excluded. No simulator call, no neorl2 import, in this stage.
5. **Freeze:** the prep kernel writes `frozen_config.json` (identified A, B, qp, qv, K, F, the grid and every candidate's val score) and its sha256. The lead commits that file and the sha into `protocol/` and TEST-LOG **before** the g0 and final kernels are pushed; the final kernel aborts if its configured sha differs from the sha of the file it loads.
6. **Evaluation:** the official simulator, seeded per episode as in section 2, 1000 episodes per seed set; the same episodes are scored by the numpy replica (`src/nd/env.py`), which copies the official random-number order (8 task draws, then targets, then starts) and the float32 reward.
7. **Reporting:** every simulator contact has a row in TEST-LOG; the numbers reported are mean, per-seed bootstrap intervals, per-episode standard deviation, and the mean +- sd over the three seed means.
Secondary, not part of any claim: the same frozen controller in mode `test` (mass 30), reported as a distribution-shift row; the oracle (true-parameter design with the frozen qp, qv) and a no-feed-forward ablation, as references.

## 5. Gates (G0) and stop rules
G0 runs before any candidate is scored on the simulator; G0a and G0b are hard, G0c is report-only (Amendment 1, A1.5).
- **G0a (arithmetic, prep kernel).** From the train file: rows 100,000; episodes 1000 x 100 rows (by the `truncated` or `done` flag; if both are all zero, consecutive blocks of 100 whose `next_obs` equals the next row's `obs`); all |action| at most 1; sum(reward) / number of trajectories equals 494.4527 within 0.05, i.e. the normalised data score is 56.64 within 0.01 (BARS.md: `report_result.py`). Also stored reward equals the DMSD reward recomputed from `next_obs` (max absolute difference 1e-4). Failure: stop; no identification result is used.
- **G0b (simulator equivalence, g0 kernel).** On 50 seeds (`5,000,000 + i`) the numpy replica and the official package give the same return, for three fixed deterministic policies (zero action; a PD law `clip(3 (tgt - pos) - 2 vel, -1, 1)`; bang-bang `sign(tgt - pos)`), at most 0.001 absolute difference per episode, and the state trajectories agree to 1e-9 on 5 episodes; the three policies in mode `test` on 20 seeds agree as well. The official package is the pinned commit with the three file hashes of `protocol/sources.sha256` (dmsd.py, info.py, dmsd_reward.py); a hash mismatch stops the kernel. Failure: stop and report; the replica is retired and nothing is scored.
- **G0c (scale anchor, g0 kernel). [REPORT-ONLY since Amendment 1, A1.5: the band below is recorded but never stops the track.]** Behaviour cloning: our own MLP 6-256-256-2 (tanh output), Adam 3e-4, batch 256, 30 epochs on the train file, final epoch, scored on the official simulator on seed-7 episodes (1000). Pass band **51.64 to 70.09** (the data score 56.64 minus 5 to the published best-of-grid BC 65.09 plus 5). This is a sanity check of the scorer's scale, not a replication of the OfflineRL BC (that package was not run). Outside the band: stop and report.
- **Identification gate (prep kernel).** Maximum absolute one-step prediction error of the identified model on the val file at most 1e-3, intercept (audit fit) at most 1e-4 in absolute value, regressor condition number reported. Failure: stop; the dynamics are not linear time-invariant in the data and the design stops there, with the finding reported.
- **Stop rules.** Any stage failing twice for an infrastructure reason (kernel error, install failure) stops the track; one re-push per stage is allowed for an infrastructure cause announced in STATUS.md first. Kaggle wall caps are in section 6. A hash or sha mismatch stops. Nothing is pushed by an agent.

## 6. Compute (own ledger)
- **Billed GPU-hours cap: 0.0.** This track needs no GPU: identification, design and a 100-step linear simulator run on CPU, and G0c is a 0.1 M-parameter MLP on CPU. `results/gpu_ledger.csv` exists with its header and stays without rows; **any GPU push is a violation of this pre-registration** and voids the claim. No GPU kernel is built (kernels/VERSIONS.md says so).
- Kaggle CPU (4 vCPU, free, private kernels `bench-neorl2-dmsd-*`, slot protocol `locks/kaggle-cpu-N`): at most 3.0 CPU-hours in total on all stages together; wall caps per kernel: dry run 900 s, prep 1,800 s, g0 3,600 s, final 3,600 s. Internet is used only to read the public pinned Hub files and the pinned GitHub commit; no secret and no token is used (the dataset is not gated).
- Mac: nothing is trained or loaded; local checks run only under `bin/guard.sh`, numpy and stdlib only.

## 7. If the claim is missed
Everything is reported: the G0 values, the per-seed means and intervals, the identified A and B, the frozen gains, the validation table, the oracle and the ablation. No gain is re-tuned after the first simulator contact; a changed design is a post-hoc variant, labelled exploratory, and it cannot support the claim. A new design needs an amendment committed first and a new set of TEST-LOG rows, and the first result stays in the record. The honest sentence in that case is the number with its interval and "did not clear the pre-registered margin over the bar" (or "G0 not reproduced: ..."); the target is not lowered.

**A1.2 Data used.** Everywhere the claim or the method is described: "the official train file (100,000 transitions) and the official validation file (20,000 transitions; used only for model-rollout selection)". **A1.3 Claim-rule completeness is hard-coded, not configurable.** `nd.stats.claim_rule` requires, inside the function: seed sets exactly {7, 42, 210}; exactly 1000 episodes in each; exactly 10,000 bootstrap draws; lower bound above 80.95; G0a and G0b passed; the frozen sha matched; replica/official difference at most 0.001. **A1.4 The rule paragraph.** The "cannot pass by chance" sentence and its probability are withdrawn. The bar (78.65) is the best of 4 hyper-parameter cells x 3 seeds, tuned on the evaluation simulator (BARS.md); the margin 2.30 is a three-seed sample sd, not a standard error. Prior witnesses put a true-parameter controller at 95 to 98 against that bar, so the rule can fail only through a scorer or replica defect or a mis-identification, not through sampling noise; passing it is the expected outcome and is reported as such.

**A1.5 G0c is report-only.** The band 51.64 to 70.09 is data score minus 5 to the published best-of-grid BC plus 5: the 5-point margins are arbitrary and the BC is our own implementation, not the OfflineRL one, so the band cannot be justified from the paper, and a legitimate BC of this mixed-gain PID data can exceed 70. G0 therefore consists of G0a and G0b (hard); G0c is recorded (`g0c.in_band`, `report_only`) and a value outside the band is reported, not a stop. G0c scores on the same seed-7 episodes as P1; this is harmless (BC does not feed the controller and the freeze precedes it) and is recorded in TEST-LOG row G7.

**A1.6 Identification check in the regime the controller visits.** The true one-step error under the LQR policy cannot be measured offline: no data exist under that policy, and the only way to measure it is the simulator, which prep never touches. What is added instead, all report-only, all in the prep report: (a) `regime_check`: the (state, action) regressors the selected closed loop visits in the identified model on the 200 validation episodes, compared with the training regressors by leverage h(z) = z^T (X^T X)^-1 z (ratio of the visited maximum and 99th percentile to the training ones; share of visited rows above the training maximum; per-coordinate range excess), together with the regressor condition number and the one-step errors already gated; (b) in the final kernel, `model_vs_simulator`: the model-rollout normalised return of the selected gain on the validation episodes versus the official-simulator P means; and the oracle row R (true-parameter design, same qp and qv) against P. Outside the data's support (a visited leverage ratio far above 1, or a large model-versus-simulator gap) is read after the fact as a reliability warning; it does not change the claim rule.

**A1.7 Wall caps and slots.** Each kernel arms an in-kernel timer (`SIGALRM`, `CFG.wall_cap_s`: dry run 900 s, prep 1,800 s, g0 3,600 s, final 3,600 s) that ends the script with an error, so the Kaggle session ends. `watch_kernel.sh` releases the `locks/kaggle-cpu-N` slot on every exit path including a watch timeout (and says the kernel may still be running); `run_wave.sh` also releases on any exit; `slot.sh release` removes a lock only if its owner file is ours. `cpu_h` in `results/cpu_ledger.csv` is defined as **wall-clock hours of the kernel session**; the four caps sum to 9,900 s = 2.75 h, within the 3.0 h budget, and a re-push is allowed only if the budget still holds with the first run's actual wall time counted.
